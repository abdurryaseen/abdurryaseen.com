From The Field

LESSONS LEARNED

What D1 Football Taught Me

Playing college football at the Division 1 level will strip you down and rebuild you. The lessons didn't stay on the field — they're the same ones running my career, my marriage, and every business I'm building today.

Discipline Beats Motivation

Motivation runs out by week three of two-a-days. What's left is the discipline to show up anyway — the 5 a.m. lifts, the film sessions, the rehab nobody's watching. That's the engine that still runs everything I do.

Earn Your Role Every Day

Nothing on a D1 roster is given. Your spot resets at every practice. Past performance doesn't guarantee today's reps — and that mindset keeps you sharp long after the jersey comes off.

Coachability Is a Superpower

The guys who lasted weren't always the most talented — they were the ones who could take hard feedback without getting defensive and actually change. That's rarer than talent, and it's what I look for now.

You Will Fail in Public

Drop a pass in an empty gym and nobody notices. Drop it in front of 60,000 people and you still have to jog back to the huddle. Recovering in public, immediately, without spiraling, is a skill most people never get forced to build.

The Team Outlasts the Individual

Stats get you noticed. Culture wins championships. I learned to measure my value by what I added to the group, not just what showed up on my own line.

Protect Your Time

Between classes, lifts, practice, and film, there was no slack in the day. The people who "have it together" aren't working more hours — they're protecting their time better than everyone else.

You Are Not Your Position

The hardest opponent wasn't on the schedule — it was the day the sport ended and I had to figure out who I was without a helmet on. Building an identity bigger than any one role is a lesson worth learning before it's forced on you.

Discomfort Isn't Danger

You push through things in sport you'd never tolerate in regular life. Learning to separate discomfort from real danger is a distinction that's served me in business and in life ever since.

THE FIELD NEVER LEAVES YOU

Every lesson above is why I built By Athletes For Athletes — so the next generation doesn't have to learn these the hard way, alone.
